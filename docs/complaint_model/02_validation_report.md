## 2 · Assessment narrative

The two-stage complaint classification model under review employs a stage-1 TF-IDF classifier, with logistic regression and xgboost as the competing models, and a stage-2 retrieval-augmented generation pipeline (rag_llm) as the final decision-maker. The model's performance is evaluated using a range of metrics, including precision-recall area under the curve (pr_auc), family accuracy, and per-label recall.

Conceptually, the two-stage design is sound, as it leverages the strengths of both TF-IDF and rag_llm to classify complaints. However, the maintained assumptions are not explicitly stated, which may lead to potential issues if the model is deployed in a different context. For instance, the model assumes that the TF-IDF features are relevant and informative for the classification task, which may not hold true in all cases.

The data quality and label provenance are critical aspects of the model's performance. The weak supervision reference, where labels derive from the CFPB product/issue taxonomy plus narrative keyword rules, is a limitation of the model. This approach may lead to biased or inaccurate labels, which can negatively impact the model's performance. Furthermore, the distinction between measured agreement and true accuracy is not explicitly addressed, which may result in overestimation of the model's performance.

The outcomes analysis reveals some interesting insights. The stage-1 TF-IDF classifier performs well, with both logistic regression and xgboost achieving high pr_auc values (0.9936 and 0.988, respectively). However, the stage-2 rag_llm pipeline struggles to achieve high accuracy, with a family accuracy of 0.3861 and a macro F1 score of 0.2516. The per-label recall values also reveal some interesting patterns, with some labels (e.g., BSA_AML, FCRA_ACCURACY, and SALES_PRACTICES) achieving high recall values, while others (e.g., ECOA_ADVERSE_ACTION, FDCPA_DEBT_VALIDATION, and TILA_ORIGINATION) struggle to achieve even moderate recall values.

The model's performance is monitored using a range of metrics, including pr_auc, family accuracy, and per-label recall. However, the guardrails in place to prevent potential issues, such as overfitting or underfitting, are not explicitly stated.

The consumer-protection/fair-lending exposure of complaint routing is a critical aspect of the model's deployment. ## 4 · Key limitations (validator-confirmed)

1. **Weak labels** — ground truth derives from the CFPB issue taxonomy +
   keyword rules, not human adjudication; stage-2 "accuracy" is agreement with
   a noisy reference. Condition: human-adjudicated golden set (≥25/category).
2. **Class imbalance** — several categories (SCRA/MLA, sales practices,
   adverse action) have thin support; per-category metrics are unstable there.
3. **LLM nondeterminism & drift** — prompt/model versions must be pinned and
   re-evaluated on the golden set before any provider/model change (the system
   records provider + model in every artifact banner).
4. **Fair-lending lens** — complaint routing affects remediation speed;
   monitoring should include parity of routing outcomes across products.

## 5 · Disposition

**Approve with Conditions** — see conditions in the assessment narrative and
limitations above; re-validation triggered by model/prompt change or stage-1
PSI > 0.25.
